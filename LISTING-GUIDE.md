# Listing guide — how to write about each artwork (for Steph + Ethan)

Use this every time a new piece goes into `/admin`. Same shape, new soul.

## Title formula

`"[Name]" ([Type])` — the artwork's name first, always in quotes.

- Original: `"Harbour Light" (Original Oil Painting)`
- Print: `"Harbour Light" (Print)`

Never: medium-first titles ("Oil on canvas abstract..."), ALL CAPS, or exclamation points.

## Description template (4 short paragraphs max)

1. **First sight (1–2 sentences).** What the buyer sees first: subject, colours, mood.
   *Good: "A lone banksia against a burnt-orange sky, all texture and late light."*
2. **Meaning (1–2 sentences).** What it speaks of: strength, rest, hope, grace. One idea only.
   *Good: "It holds the feeling of standing your ground and finding it beautiful."*
3. **Craft (1 sentence).** Medium + ground + size. Facts, no adjectives.
   *Good: "Oil and mixed media on canvas, 61 × 91 cm, ready to hang."*
4. **Ownership (1 sentence).** Originals: scarcity. Prints: choice.
   - Original: "The only one in existence. When it sells, it is gone for good."
   - Print: "Printed to order in your choice of size, framed or unframed."

## House rules

- Write like you talk to a friend at an exhibition. Warm, plain words.
- "Soul", "hope", "strength", "grace", "light" are home. Doctrine words
  ("prophetic", "ministry", "scripture") stay out — the work carries the faith quietly.
- Never invent: sizes, framing, certificates, or delivery promises you haven't set up.
- No AI tells: no "It's not X, it's Y", no "no this, no that" lists, no em dashes
  in titles, no "Unlock / seamless / curated".
- One listing = one feeling. If you need two feelings, you need two sentences
  in paragraph 2, not a longer paragraph 1.

## Photos (matter as much as words)

- Daylight, no flash, straight on. Include one close-up of texture and one
  "on a wall" shot so buyers can picture the scale.
- Upload via the Photo field in `/admin`. Landscape orientation suits the grid best.
